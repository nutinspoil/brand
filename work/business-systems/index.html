One sale, fully accounted for

  1. Record the sale
  2. Split cash and Mobile Money
  3. Update inventory
  4. Close the shift
  5. Manager reviews

Every step in that loop is money that has to agree by the end of the day. When the register, the cash drawer, and the shelf tell different stories, the shortfall lands on the attendant or the owner. The system exists so those records agree shift by shift.

What existed before

Busy retail operations frequently run on handwritten counter receipts, fragmented spreadsheets, and verbal staff check-ins.

Cashiers manually calculated totals under pressure. Cash and Mobile Money payments were noted on loose paper. Owners had no reliable way to verify whether the stock on the shelf matched the money collected at close of trade.

Who the system serves

Counter cashiers — fast item search, instant price lookup, multi-tender payment records, and quick receipts under queue pressure.

Supervisors and accountants — shift handovers, approval of voided lines, live stock levels, and verified end-of-day banking summaries.

Owners — reporting, inventory visibility, and an audit history that shows who changed what, and when.

What was built

  • A high-speed counter register with barcode-scanner compatibility and rapid keyboard lookup.
  • Split payment support for Ghanaian retail: cash and Mobile Money recorded against one sale.
  • An inventory ledger that adjusts stock on sale completion instead of through a second entry.
  • Role-restricted oversight: voiding an item or editing a price requires manager authentication.
  • Shift-balancing reports comparing expected cash and MoMo totals against the counted drawer.
  • Role-based operations for orders, payments, inventory, and reporting beyond the register itself.

Decisions that shaped it

Keyboard and touch speed before visual ornament

Transitions were kept minimal and shortcuts prioritised. In internal testing, a five-item transaction completed in under 12 seconds. That figure is a build test, not a client performance claim.

Strict separation between cashier and manager

Cashiers cannot alter historical prices or void completed lines without manager approval. Manual overrides remain in an audit trail.

Blind reconciliation at shift end

Attendants count the drawer before seeing the system's expected total, so discrepancies cannot be adjusted away.

Offline is a prototype, not a promise

The register was designed with continuity in mind. A separate offline-resilient engine, using local caching and deterministic reconnection, is still under technical test. It is not presented as a released feature.

What staff can do now

Staff record sales, verify cash and Mobile Money, and close a shift with a balanced record instead of reconciling paper books later. Discrepancies between the drawer, the payment statement, and shelf stock surface at the close of each shift.

Client identities, transaction volumes, financial totals, and staff records remain confidential. This page documents method and architecture only.

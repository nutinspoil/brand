Working principle

“Custom software should earn its complexity. If an existing tool solves the core problem better, we will say so.”

The form is chosen before the stack. When a standard tool already solves the core problem, the honest answer is to say so.

Choose the form

Public, operational, or evolving.

The same business can need all three over time. The first release only needs the one that matches the current situation.

01

Public-facing products

The part of the business a customer meets without you in the room.

Best for
Guests, shoppers, clients, and visitors who need to understand, compare, and act.
Form
Websites and storefronts with structured public information and a direct next step.
What changes
A customer can answer the common questions before contact, and arrive with context when they do.
Not a fit
Not the fix when the real problem is internal reconciliation or a broken operating process.

Operational products

The systems staff use to record work, control access, and close the day.

Best for
Cashiers, supervisors, accountants, and owners who need one recorded flow.
Form
POS, shift ledgers, role-scoped operations systems, and custom internal tools.
What changes
Work leaves paper and chat and becomes a record that can be reviewed, closed, and defended.
Not a fit
Not worth building when a standard tool already matches the process and the team will use it.

New and evolving products

A product that needs a first release, a technical answer, or a new owner.

Best for
Founders and teams defining a first version or carrying a live product forward.
Form
Scoped first releases, device-focused applications, maintenance, and technical tests.
What changes
The product gets a defined next release, a tested technical direction, or a maintained handover.
Not a fit
Not a fit when the product has no named owner and no decision that a release would resolve.

The seven

Seven services, each with a boundary.

A service description is useful when it says what it builds, what changes, and when it is not the right answer.

Featured service · 01

Web development

A public website with a clear structure: what the business offers, who it serves, where it is, and how to begin.

Best for Businesses whose public presence is a profile, a social page, or an outdated site.

  • Visitors can understand the offer without a call.
  • Directions, hours, and next steps sit in one place.
  • The business stops repeating the same answers in chat.

Not the right fitNot the first form when the problem is a transaction, an order flow, or an internal reconciliation process.

Capabilities

Different ideas need different forms.

The form follows the purpose. A responsive website may be enough. A custom application may be necessary. An existing tool may be better than a custom build. The right form depends on what the idea needs to make possible.

Describe the problem → See the work →
Starting point
The practical change required, not an assumed technology stack.
Scope
A scoped first release agreed in writing. No hidden retainers or lock-in clauses.
Recommendation
The simplest form that makes the required work possible.

An existing tool may be better than a custom build

Custom software should earn its complexity. If a well-supported off-the-shelf tool solves your requirement with less expense and lower long-term maintenance, we will say so during discovery. We build bespoke only when standard products fail to address the core problem.

Three kinds of problem

Most requests arrive as one of three situations. The situation decides the starting form; the discovery decides the scope.

01 · Public-facing

Customers cannot find, understand, trust, or act on what you offer.

Websites, online stores, digital identities, and public platforms that carry the offer clearly.

↓
02 · Operational

Daily work depends on paper, spreadsheets, and disconnected records.

POS, business systems, dashboards, and workflow software that keep the record accurate.

↓
03 · New and evolving

An idea needs a useful first release, or a product needs its next capability.

Custom software, prototypes, first releases, and maintenance for products already in use.

↓

Public-facing products

Digital experiences that present an organisation, its products, or its services to the world with clarity and credibility.

Typically includes

  • Business, corporate, and institutional websites
  • Online stores and commerce experiences
  • Service information architecture and enquiry paths
  • Digital identities and public platforms

Best for

Businesses, institutions, founders, and creative organisations that need customers, partners, or audiences to understand what they offer and take action.

What changes

People can discover what you do, understand rates or offerings, browse products, and make contact without repeated manual back-and-forth.

Operational products

Internal tools, point-of-sale systems, and business software engineered to keep daily routines accurate, recorded, and manageable.

Typically includes

  • Point-of-sale and counter systems
  • Business management systems and internal tools
  • Role-based access, approvals, and audit records
  • Stock, orders, reporting, and workflow software

Best for

Store attendants, operations managers, administrative teams, and business owners managing transactions, records, and multi-step staff workflows.

What changes

Sales, stock, and cash records agree at the end of the day; handoffs stop living in paper books, spreadsheets, and chat threads.

New and evolving products

Custom software, prototypes, first-release applications, and continuous technical improvements for ventures solving novel problems.

Typically includes

  • Custom software and purpose-built applications
  • Product prototypes and useful first releases
  • Applications assessed across phone, tablet, and desktop
  • Maintenance, fixes, and focused improvements

Best for

Founders, innovators, and established organisations launching new ventures, or teams whose operational constraints cannot fit standard SaaS tools.

What changes

An ambition becomes a working first release with agreed boundaries, instead of a specification that never ships.

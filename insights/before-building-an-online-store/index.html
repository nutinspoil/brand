What to decide before building an online store

An online store depends on more than product pages. Planning the catalogue, fulfilment, and customer communication defines the project.

Map the product path

A store succeeds when the route from product discovery to delivery is clear. Map that route before choosing a platform, and note who is responsible at each step.

  • How products will be grouped and described
  • How availability and prices will be maintained
  • Which payment providers are appropriate
  • How delivery, collection, and returns work
  • Who manages orders and customer questions

If any of those answers is “someone will handle it”, the store is not ready to be built. The gap will surface as a delayed order, not as a design problem.

Choose launch essentials

Start with product pages, checkout, and order confirmation. Add promotions, complex stock automation, or loyalty features only when the underlying process is clear and someone owns it.

A smaller catalogue with accurate information is more credible than a large catalogue with missing prices. Launch with what can be kept current.

Agree what happens after an order

The order confirmation is not the end of the journey. Decide how the customer is told about dispatch, how changes are handled, and who answers when delivery is late. These decisions determine whether the storefront reduces work or simply moves it.

Confirm the providers

Payment, delivery, and stock handling depend on providers and business processes. Confirm them before describing them as available features. A store that promises a payment method the business cannot support creates the exact friction it was meant to remove.

One caution

Do not assume payment and delivery options are included. They depend on providers and on the business process behind them.

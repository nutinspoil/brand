Why a growing business may need a POS system

A point-of-sale system is useful when it makes a real sales workflow clearer. Start by mapping the work before choosing features.

Start a conversation ↗All guides ↗
Subject
POS & operations
Written for
Owners and staff

Map a sale end to end.

A POS choice should follow the sales workflow, not a feature list.

Trace a sale through item entry, payment, receipt, stock adjustment and daily close. Note which steps are manual or easy to get wrong.

1Trace the sales workflow

  • Understand the sales process
  • List the records staff need to keep
  • Identify who needs access to which tasks
  • Decide which reports help people make decisions
  • Confirm devices, connectivity and payment requirements

2Start with daily essentials

Begin with sales, payment and close-out. Add stock, refunds, offline selling or branches only where staff need them.

3How SAD can help with POS

SAD can map counter workflows, staff roles and reporting needs, then confirm device and payment-provider requirements before build.

One caution.

Do not assume

Do not assume every POS includes inventory, offline operation, refunds or branch tools. Confirm the feature set for the specific implementation.
